Privacy Policy

PJ Talk — messages for two · Effective November 15, 2026

The short version: PJ Talk doesn't collect your data. Your conversation lives in iCloud, in a space shared with the one person you invite, and PJ Talk has no server and no database of its own. The only time it talks to anything other than iCloud is to fetch the preview of a web link in a message, and that request goes from your device to that website. PJ Talk does keep a short health report (numbers, codes and times, never a message) in the conversation's iCloud space, where the other person's PJ Talk can read it; a switch turns it off. No ads, no analytics, no tracking.

Who we are, and who PJ Talk is for

PJ Talk is made by Jack McVickar, an independent developer in Illinois, USA. It is a private messaging app for two people: the person who starts a conversation and the one person they invite. It is currently in testing through Apple's TestFlight. Questions about this policy: support@pjjournal.com.

What PJ Talk stores, and where

  • Your conversation (texts, photos, videos, voice messages and their transcripts, reactions, edits, the two of you's names, profile pictures, and anything you put in Our album) is stored in Apple's iCloud, in the iCloud account of the person who started the conversation.
  • It is shared with one invited person through iCloud sharing. The invitation is a private link; nobody else can join, and nothing is ever public. Each conversation is its own shared space.
  • Locked fields: the words of each message, who sent it, reactions, edit history and transcripts; your names and chat background; and in Our album the caption, title, words, place, file name and who added it. These are written as encrypted fields, which iCloud protects end to end when Advanced Data Protection is on for the account. Photos, videos and recordings themselves travel and rest encrypted under Apple's iCloud protections. If you want everything in the conversation, files included, encrypted end to end, turn on Advanced Data Protection for your iCloud account (Settings > your name > iCloud > Advanced Data Protection); then not even Apple can read it. Housekeeping details ride alongside unlocked so the phones can sync: when a message was sent, what kind it is, which message it replies to, when it was read or played, and a file's size and length.
  • Favorites and Most special marks (the star and the heart on a photo, video or voice message) are stored with the conversation, so the other person can see which items you marked. See "Most special and Face ID" below for what you can keep to your own phone.
  • Your iPhone keeps recent photos and videos on the phone (by default the full-size files used in the last 14 days, up to 2 GB); older ones stay in iCloud and download again when you tap them. It also keeps the list of your conversations and its own copy of the messages, so they show without a connection. A voice message you are still recording, or haven't sent yet, is kept on your phone only until you send or discard it.
  • The Mac app keeps a full offline copy of the conversation in a folder you choose on that Mac, as a backup. It keeps every version of a message it has seen and notes when one was deleted. Nothing in it goes anywhere else.

We can't see any of it: there is no PJ Talk server, and your iCloud account is yours. Apple's handling of iCloud data is covered by Apple's Privacy Policy.

App health: what your device reports, and to whom

So that a problem is noticed early, each device PJ Talk runs on writes one small health report into the conversation's iCloud space, next to the messages. It is rewritten in place (about every half hour while the app is in use, and when its state changes), not added to.

  • Who can read it: the two people in the conversation, through PJ Talk, and nobody else. It does not come to us: there is no PJ Talk server. Each person's PJ Talk shows both people's reports in Settings → Health (on the Mac: Window → Health), and the Mac app can show the person using it a notice when a device in the conversation has been having trouble for a while. That notice is made on the Mac from the report; nothing is sent to do it.
  • What is in it: numbers, codes and times only. The kind of device (iPhone or Mac; for a Mac, its model and processor), the PJ Talk build number and the system version; whether the app is working normally, and a short code for why not; when it last synced; how many syncs failed and the last error code; how many times iCloud asked the app to slow down; how many messages are waiting to send and how old the oldest is; how many times in the last day the app crashed, hung or was closed by the system, the name of the start-up step it had reached and the memory it was using; the start and end times of recent trouble; how many records it still has to read; and the sending summary described next.
  • The sending summary: for the last seven days, how many texts, photos, videos and voice messages were sent and how many photos, videos and voice messages were downloaded, by kind of connection (Wi-Fi, cellular, other), with the typical and the slowest time and speed; and for the few slowest of the last day, the kind, the size in megabytes, the length in seconds, the video quality, the kind of connection and how long it took. It holds no message, no file name, and nothing that points to a particular message.
  • What is never in it: a message, a name, a photo, a place, a file name or any text you wrote. The crash and hang counts come from Apple's on-device diagnostics (MetricKit); PJ Talk keeps only the kind, the count and the time from them, never a crash log.
  • You can see all of it: Settings → Health → "What is shared" lists every value exactly as it is written.
  • The switch: "Share app health" in Settings → Health. It is on to begin with. Turned off, your device writes one report that says only "off" (with the build number, device kind and system version) and then nothing more; the other person can see that it is off.
  • How long it is kept: there is one report per person per kind of device and each new one replaces the last, so iCloud holds only the latest. The Mac app also keeps the latest report it has seen from each device in its own folder on that Mac. Both go when the conversation's iCloud data, or the app, is deleted.

What we collect

Nothing, automatically. PJ Talk contains no analytics, advertising, crash-reporting or tracking tools, no third-party code and no outside software kits; it is built only on Apple's own frameworks. The health report above stays between the two people in the conversation and is not sent to us. We don't share or sell any data. We only receive what you choose to send:

  • Problem reports. "Report a problem" opens an email to us with a short diagnostics summary (app version, device, sync and recording status, the health and sending numbers described above, where sound was playing). It contains none of your messages' words or pictures, and you see it before you send it. "Copy diagnostics" only puts that same summary on your clipboard.
  • Beta testing. If you test PJ Talk through Apple's TestFlight, Apple may share your name or email, crash reports, and any feedback you submit with us, under Apple's terms.

We use these only to answer you and fix problems, keep them only as long as needed, and delete them on request.

Where PJ Talk connects

  • iCloud, for everything above: your messages, sharing, and the notification that a new message is waiting.
  • Web links. When a message contains a link, your device fetches that page's title and picture to show a preview card, the way Messages does. That request goes from your device straight to that website, so the site sees a visit from you, as it would when you open the link. There is no preview service in between, and the preview is kept on your device only, not in iCloud, and each device fetches its own. There is no setting to turn previews off yet.

That is all. Transcription, search, link previews and everything else that could involve your words runs on your device. To choose a video's quality PJ Talk looks at whether the device is on Wi-Fi or cellular; that is read on the device and goes nowhere except, as a word ("wifi", "cellular"), into the sending summary above.

Permissions PJ Talk asks for

  • Camera and microphone: to record the photos, videos and voice messages you send. Only while you record.
  • Speech recognition: to turn your voice messages into words. This happens on your iPhone, using Apple's on-device recognizer; the sound never leaves your phone for this and there is no fallback to a server. Your own words are saved with the message in iCloud so the other person can read them. Words made on your phone for the other person's messages stay on your phone. Both are optional, in Settings → Transcripts.
  • Photos: choosing a photo or video to send, or to add to Our album, uses Apple's photo picker, so PJ Talk only sees the items you pick and needs no access to your library. "Save to Photos" asks for add-only access, which lets PJ Talk add to your library without seeing it. Our album's "Remove from Photos", which deletes the originals you already brought into the album, asks for access to your library when you use it, and that is the only time PJ Talk asks. If you have given that access, PJ Talk also uses it for one more thing: when you add a picture that carries no date of its own, it looks up the date Photos has for that one picture. It never looks through your library.
  • Face ID: only if you turn on "Ask for Face ID" for Most special (below). The check is done by iOS; PJ Talk learns only whether it passed and never sees your face data or your passcode.
  • Notifications: a new message arrives as a plain "New message" alert; the words shown on your Lock Screen are fetched from iCloud by your phone, and "Hide previews" keeps them off it. On the Mac, a small helper that is part of PJ Talk runs when a message notification arrives: it asks iCloud whether there is anything new from the other person in the last few minutes and, if there is not (for example it was your own message, sent from your iPhone), makes the alert silent. It only reads, saves nothing, and talks to nothing but iCloud. The recording timer on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island is made on your phone and never pushed anywhere.

PJ Talk never asks for your contacts, location, calendar or health data. Every permission can be changed any time in Settings.

What the other person can see

Only what you choose: whether you're in the app, when you're typing or recording, your last active time, and when you've read or played their messages. Each of these is a switch in Settings → Privacy, and the other person's phone honours it.

Most special and Face ID

A favorite (star) and a Most special (heart) mark are part of the conversation, so both of you can see them. Two optional settings in Settings → Privacy apply to your own iPhone only, are stored only on that iPhone, and are not told to the other person:

  • Keep Most special out of the main library: your Most special items then show only under the Most special filter on your phone, and their marks are not shown in the chat on your phone.
  • Ask for Face ID to open Most special: iOS checks Face ID (or Touch ID) before the filter opens. If that does not succeed, iOS offers the device passcode instead, so anyone who knows the phone's passcode can open it. If the phone has no passcode at all, there is nothing to check and it opens.

These settings hide things on your phone's screen. They do not add encryption, and they do not change what the other person sees on theirs.

Your choices and your data

  • Unsent messages (waiting or "Not delivered") can be deleted; they leave your phone and were never in iCloud.
  • Delivered messages can be edited, and their words corrected, with the history kept. There is no delete for a delivered message yet: a conversation is a shared record for both of you, and removing one person's half of it is not something this version does.
  • Our album items can be deleted by either of you; the item and its file are removed from iCloud and from both phones on the next sync.
  • Leaving: the invited person can leave a conversation, which removes it from their iCloud and phone and leaves the owner's copy untouched. The owner can delete a conversation nobody has joined. A conversation both of you are in can be hidden from the list; deleting it for both is not offered.
  • How long things are kept: PJ Talk has no expiry of its own. The conversation, Our album and the latest health reports stay in iCloud until one of you removes them as described here. On the iPhone, full-size photos and videos not used lately are cleared from the phone by themselves (they stay in iCloud); link previews and thumbnails are in a cache that iOS may clear at any time.
  • App health: turn off "Share app health" in Settings → Health at any time (see above).
  • Delete everything: delete PJ Talk from your devices, and remove its data from iCloud in Settings → [your name] → iCloud → Manage Storage. The conversation itself is stored in the iCloud account of the person who started it, so if you were invited, leaving removes your access and your copy, and their copy stays theirs. The Mac's offline copy is a folder on that Mac; delete it there.

Future optional features

If PJ Talk ever adds a feature that sends anything outside your devices and iCloud, it will be off unless you turn it on, and we'll update this policy first to explain exactly what it does.

Children

PJ Talk is not directed to children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect information from them.
