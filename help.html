01 · Getting started

Three ways to capture a thought

iPhone · iPad · Mac

However you open PJ on your iPhone, iPad, or Mac, there are three things you can do — talk, write, and read back:

  • Talk — just speak, and PJ writes it down for you, on your device, saving the moment you stop. On your iPhone it's Start talking at the bottom of your journal; on the Mac it's Talk at the top.
  • Write — type an entry instead, like a note. On the iPhone, Type instead; on the Mac, Write.
  • Read — your journal, newest first, with search and labels. On the iPhone this is simply the screen you open to; on the Mac it's Read at the top.

Your Apple Watch does one of these — talk. Raise your wrist, record a thought, and PJ saves it and syncs it to your other devices. Reading and writing your journal happen on iPhone, iPad, and Mac. See Apple Watch below.

On iPad: PJ runs on iPad too — talk, write, and read all work, with a proper iPad layout that holds whichever way you turn the screen and a comfortable reading column on the bigger display. If anything ever looks off, tell me at support@pjjournal.com.

A quick setup check (new in build 34). When PJ needs it, a short, friendly checklist helps make sure the things that let it work and sync are in place — that you’re signed in to iCloud, that you’re online for the first sync, and, if you’re running the iPhone version on a Mac, a pointer to the Mac version made for your computer. It never blocks you: there’s always a Start writing that takes you straight in. You can run it any time from Settings → Check my setup. If your journal is ever slow to arrive on a new device, that’s the first place to look — it’ll point you to iCloud Keychain if that’s what’s holding things up.

02 · Start from a moment or a photo

Let a moment — or a photo — start the entry

iPhone · iPad

On iPhone and iPad there’s another way to begin: let a recent moment start the entry for you. On the writing screen tap Start from a moment, and your phone offers a few recent moments — a photo, a walk, a place you went, something you listened to. Pick one and PJ opens a new entry already started with a warm line or two, ready for you to keep writing in your own words. Nothing is saved until you tap Save.

Start from a photo (new in build 30, iPhone and iPad). Right there on the writing screen is a second way in — Start from a photo — which opens your photo library so you can pick any picture to begin an entry, handy for when the moment you want isn’t in Apple’s suggestions. PJ reads the photo’s date and, if it has one, its location, keeps a small private copy the same way, and opens a new entry for you to write. It asks for no photo-library permission — you hand it just the one picture you pick.

On the save screen you decide what comes in, with three small switches PJ remembers:

  • Date it — dates the entry to when the moment happened (on by default).
  • Include who & where — off by default; brings in the fuller description (which can include the place and the names of people in the photo) and adds a small place-and-date line to the entry.
  • Keep a small picture of the moment — off by default; keeps a small, private copy of the photo right in the entry, so you remember what it was about. It’s a downsized copy with the location and camera data stripped out, synced across your own devices, and shown on iPhone, iPad, and Mac.

New in build 30: if a moment (or the photo you picked) has more than one picture, PJ shows a small row of them so you choose which to keep — with a clear preview of exactly what will be saved. And if a picture carries a location but no place name, the entry simply notes “Location kept”, so nothing is stored that you can’t see.

Entries you start this way are gathered under From a moment — at the top of your Labels on iPhone, and in the sidebar on Mac — handy, since a moment often dates an entry back into the past where it’s otherwise easy to lose. (More in Finding entries.)

Start from a moment is iPhone and iPad only — it uses Apple’s Journaling Suggestions, which the Mac doesn’t have. The picker shows what your phone decides are recent, meaningful moments; it is not a photo browser and you can’t scroll to a specific date, so if you don’t see much, that’s normal — it fills in as you go about your days. Start from a photo (picking a photo yourself) works on iPhone, iPad, and the Mac as of build 35. Private, as always: the picture, the place, and the description only enter your journal if you choose them, nothing about a moment leaves your phone, and it’s all encrypted.

03 · Quick capture

Catch a thought in one move

iPhoneApple Watch

PJ comes with a built-in Capture a thought action that opens PJ already recording — nothing between you and the thought. It's part of the app, so there's nothing to build; just put it wherever you'll reach for it:

  • Say it — “Hey Siri, capture a thought in PJ.” Works with the phone in your pocket or on a walk.
  • Lock Screen widget — one tap from the screen you're already looking at. Long-press the Lock Screen → Customize → tap under the clock → PJ → Capture a thought.
  • Home Screen widget — long-press an empty spot → Add Widget → PJ → Capture a thought.
  • Action Button (iPhone 15 Pro and later) — Settings → Action Button → Shortcut → Capture a thought.
  • Back Tap — Settings → Accessibility → Touch → Back Tap → Double Tap → Capture a thought. Two taps on the back of the phone, even through a case.
  • Control Center — swipe down from the top-right → + → Add a control → Capture a thought.
  • Apple Watch complication — add Capture a thought to any watch face and your wrist becomes the fastest way in. In the iPhone Watch app → your face → a complication slot → under PJ, pick Capture a thought.
It still works with Private mode on. Quick capture goes straight into recording even when PJ is locked — a capture that stopped to ask for a passphrase wouldn't be quick, and the thought would be gone. You can always add while locked; reading your journal still needs the passphrase.

04 · Apple Watch

Talk to your wrist

Apple Watchneeds your iPhone

Raise your wrist, open PJ, and say what's on your mind — great on a walk or in the car. PJ records it right there on the watch, saves it, and syncs it to your iPhone, iPad, and Mac.

The watch is for capturing, not reading. You can record a thought from your wrist anytime — but reading back through your journal lives on your iPhone, iPad, and Mac, where there's room for it.
Works on Apple Watch Series 4 or newer (including both SE models and the Ultra) — that's what watchOS 10 needs. A Series 3 or older can't run the watch app, but everything else in PJ still works on your phone.

To add the watch app:

  1. Install PJ on your iPhone and open it once.
  2. On the iPhone, open the built-in Watch app.
  3. Scroll to Available Apps, find PJ, tap Install.
  4. Give it a minute — it lands on your watch, and you're set.

Not showing up? Keep the watch nearby and charging, confirm PJ is on the phone and opened once, then check Available Apps again. Still stuck — email me your watch model at support@pjjournal.com.

05 · Finding entries

Getting around your journal

iPhone · Mac
  • Scroll the timeline — the day you're reading stays pinned at the top, so you always know where you are.
  • Jump — go straight to your Oldest or Newest entry, or browse by year and month.
  • On this day — see what you wrote on today's date in past years.
  • Search any word, or filter by a label.
  • Pin an entry to the top (new in build 32) — got one you keep coming back to? Swipe it, press and hold, or open it and tap Pin, and it moves into a Pinned group at the top of your journal. Unpin to send it back to its date. Pins sync across your devices, and it’s its own thing — editing an entry’s words or labels never changes whether it’s pinned.
  • A photo on the row (new in build 32) — an entry that kept a picture now shows a small thumbnail of it in your timeline and lists, so you can spot it at a glance. (If the picture is still syncing over from another device, you’ll see a small photo icon until it arrives.)

Mac  On the Mac, press Esc to close an entry and return to the overview, where your total entry count lives.

06 · If a recording didn't get written down

Nothing with audio is ever lost

iPhone · Mac

Once in a while a recording doesn't transcribe on the first try. PJ keeps retrying, and gathers any that still need it in one place.

  1. Look for Recordings still needing words — in Settings, and on the badge at the top-left of your timeline.
  2. Open one, tap Listen, then Write it down again.

07 · Deleting

Deleting, and changing your mind

iPhone · Mac

Deleting takes two deliberate taps and always moves an entry to Recently Deleted first — nothing disappears by accident.

  • Restore anything from Settings → Recently Deleted, whenever you like.
  • Delete permanently a single entry when you want that one gone for good.
  • Empty Recently Deleted clears the whole bin at once.

A permanent delete is final and clears from all your devices. PJ never auto-deletes on a timer — things wait until you decide.

08 · Private mode

An extra lock, only if you want it

iPhone · Mac

PJ is already encrypted and private to you. Private mode is an optional, stronger layer for anyone who wants a passphrase on their journal.

Lock vs. Private mode

Lock is a quick screen cover you can tap anytime. Private mode (Settings → Private mode) puts a passphrase on your whole journal — nothing opens without it, on any device. When you turn it on you'll save a one-time recovery code; keep it somewhere safe, it's the only way back in if you forget the passphrase.

Leave Private mode off unless you want it. If you do turn it on, get all your devices on the latest PJ first.

09 · Your old journal

Bring years of history in

Mac for OneNoteiPhone · Mac for a ready folder
Importing from OneNote needs a Mac. PJ turns a OneNote "Export to Word" file into your journal on the Mac. If your entries are already a folder or .zip of dated notes, you can import those on the iPhone too — but the OneNote conversion is Mac-only.

Already journaled somewhere else? PJ can bring it in so your whole history lives in one place.

  1. Mac  In PJ, open Import History and point it at your OneNote export (or a folder / .zip of dated notes).
  2. PJ shows a preview first — how many entries, what dates — before anything is added.
  3. Import. If it's not right, there's a one-tap Undo.

Every export is a little different — if you've got an old journal you want in PJ, just text me and I'll walk through it with you.

10 · Troubleshooting

If something's acting up

iPhone · Mac · Watch

PJ is still young, so now and then something gets stuck. The fixes below are safe to try: your entries are encrypted and kept in your own Apple iCloud — the same iCloud that already holds your Photos and Notes — so reinstalling PJ never loses anything and never exposes anything. Your journal comes right back, for your eyes only.

The mic won't start when you tap Talk

Usually this is iOS getting its microphone permission into a muddle — Settings can even show PJ's mic switched on while the app still can't open it. A clean reinstall resets it:

  1. Delete PJ from your iPhone (press and hold the icon → Remove App → Delete App).
  2. Reopen TestFlight and install PJ again.
  3. Open PJ, tap Talk, and tap Allow when it asks for the microphone.

Your entries come straight back from your own iCloud — you won't lose anything, and nothing of yours leaves your devices. If you use Private mode, have your passphrase handy for the first open after reinstalling.

You added PJ on a Mac and it's empty

If PJ on your Mac shows nothing while your iPhone is full, it's almost always one switch — and your journal is safe. The Mac is simply waiting for the key that unlocks it.

  1. Sign in to the Mac as you — the same Apple Account as your iPhone (Apple menu → System Settings → your name at the top). PJ only ever uses your iCloud, so a Mac signed in as someone else shows an empty journal.
  2. Turn on iCloud Keychain — on both the iPhone and the Mac. This is the one PJ needs, and it's separate from Photos and iCloud Drive, so everything else can sync while PJ still can't. On each device: Settings (Mac: System Settings) → your name → iCloud → Passwords & Keychain → on. It has to be on in both places — your phone holds the key, and iCloud Keychain is how it reaches the Mac.
  3. Open PJ and give it a few minutes. The key arrives through iCloud and your journal fills in on its own. If you use Private mode, PJ asks for your passphrase first.

Still empty after signing in as yourself with Keychain on in both places? Tap Share diagnostics in PJ (it carries no journal text) and email it to support@pjjournal.com — it shows me exactly what PJ is waiting on.

A quicker first thing to try for most hiccups: fully close PJ (swipe it away from the app switcher) and reopen it. If that doesn't do it, a reinstall like the one above clears most stubborn states.

When something's off, the single most useful thing you can send me is what you were doing right when it happened — "I tapped Talk and it just spun," "it happened right after the app updated." That, plus a screenshot and TestFlight's Send Feedback, is what lets me pin it down fast.

This list grows as we find things together — so if you hit something that isn't here, that's exactly the kind of thing to tell me.

11 · Privacy

It's yours, and it stays that way

Every device

It's the same security you already trust every day. PJ has no server and no database of its own — it only uses your own Apple iCloud, the same place your Photos, Notes, and Messages already live. Your entries are encrypted on your device and turned into words on your device (never in the cloud), and they sync only across your devices through your iCloud.

The key that unlocks your journal stays in your iCloud Keychain and on your devices — so your entries can be read only by you, on your own devices. Nothing you write is ever sent to the developer or to anyone else. That's the whole point of PJ: your journaling gets the same protection as the rest of your Apple life, and nothing more is asked of you.
